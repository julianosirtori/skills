# Shooting feedback

Editing rescues a little; shooting right fixes everything. Turn the analyze
summary into two or three concrete changes, starting with the one that cost the
most photos. Written for an entry APS-C body with a kit zoom (the Canon R50 with
the RF-S 18-45 f/4.5-6.3 is the reference), shooting JPEG.

## Read the summary like this

| What the numbers show | What to tell the user |
|---|---|
| Shutter 1/250 to 1/400 on most photos, many `soft` or smeared limbs | Runners need **1/1000 s** (1/800 at the very least). At 1/250 arms and feet blur and faces soften. Use Tv (shutter priority) at 1/1000 or M at 1/1000 with Auto ISO. |
| ISO 100-400 in daylight with a slow shutter | There was light to spare: the camera chose a low ISO because the shutter was slow. ISO 800-1600 at 1/1000 looks far better than ISO 200 with motion blur. On this sensor ISO 3200 is still sellable at web size. |
| Same shutter, ISO jumps a lot between frames | Fine if it is Auto ISO in Tv or M. Set an Auto ISO ceiling of 6400. |
| `color-cast` on most photos (purple or blue asphalt) | Auto white balance gets fooled by blue sky and shade. Set WB to **Daylight** (sun) or **Shade** and keep it for the whole session: every photo comes out the same and editing is one sync. |
| Many `blown-subject` or a lot of clipping | Expose for faces, not the scene. In hard sun use exposure compensation -1/3 to -2/3 and, if the menu has it, Highlight Tone Priority (D+), which protects white shirts and skin. |
| Many `contraluz` | Change position so the **sun is behind you** or to your side. A backlit runner on a JPEG is almost always a reject. |
| Many bursts of 4+ frames | Shoot short bursts (2-4 frames) at the right moment instead of long ones. Less culling, same keepers. |
| Lots of photos with tiny runners | Get closer or zoom to 45 mm, and shoot runners at 3-10 m. A buyer pays for their face, not the avenue. |
| `slow-shutter` with `soft` concentrated in a time block | Light dropped (clouds, shade). Watch the ISO; raise the ceiling instead of letting the shutter drop. |

## Settings that work (starting point)

- **Mode:** Tv 1/1000 s (or M 1/1000 s, f/5.6-6.3, Auto ISO up to 6400).
- **AF:** Servo AF, whole-area tracking with people and eye detection on. Pick
  the runner early and follow.
- **Drive:** high-speed continuous with the mechanical or electronic first
  curtain shutter. The fully electronic shutter can bend fast arms and legs
  (rolling shutter); avoid it for runners.
- **WB:** fixed (Daylight or Shade), not Auto.
- **Picture style:** Standard, sharpness at default. Skip in-camera "vivid"
  looks; they clip colors like pink and red shirts that the editor cannot undo.
- **File:** JPEG Large Fine is right for volume event work. RAW (or C-RAW) only
  for difficult light, and only with a fast card.
- **Lens:** at 45 mm and f/6.3 the background is busy. Stand where the
  background is far away and plain (trees in shade, not cars and tents). A
  cheap fast prime (an RF 50 mm f/1.8) gives faster shutter speeds, cleaner
  backgrounds and sharper faces in parks; a longer zoom (RF-S 55-210) lets you
  shoot from farther away on the course.

## Blurred backgrounds without a new lens

Runners love a blurred background. With an f/6.3 kit zoom it has to come from
somewhere else:

- **Distance:** zoom to 45 mm, stand so the runner is 3-5 m away and the
  background 20 m or more behind them.
- **Panning:** 1/60-1/125 s, follow the runner smoothly with the camera at
  their speed and keep following after the shutter. The background streaks,
  the runner stays sharp. Hit rate is low at first (expect 1 in 3), so shoot
  short bursts. If the frames show smeared faces, pan more smoothly or go
  faster (1/125-1/160).
- **A fast prime** (f/1.8) is the real fix; `bg_blur` in editing is the
  fallback, and it only looks right on clean, separated runners.

## Position and timing

- Sun behind or beside you; never shoot into it.
- Pick spots where runners come toward you on a straight, with a clean
  background, ideally where they smile: after a turn, near the finish, at a
  water station.
- A slightly low angle (crouching) makes runners look strong; shooting down
  from above makes them look short and tired.
- Catch the stride with both feet off the ground or the front foot just landing.
